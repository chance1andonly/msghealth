# Story selection (owner direction 2026-11-26): tie to the product AND be gripping

The story must be a mirror of what MsgHealth does (see PRODUCT.md: texting that keeps regulars and wins back lapsed clients for Atlanta hairstylists/barbers). Pick stories where the MORAL is the product's job, so the last-10-seconds pivot feels earned, not stapled on.

## Must-pass test (all three, or drop the idea)
1. MORAL: the story's lesson is one of: people drift away silently / nobody noticed they left / one timely message would have changed it / the loyal customers were the whole business / reaching out first wins people back / a small habit compounds / a forgotten follow-up cost a fortune. Write the moral in one sentence; if it needs "kind of", drop it.
2. PIVOT: "that is what Message Health does for a barber's regulars" must follow in one line without a stretch. If the pivot would work for any product, the story is too generic.
3. GRIP: a shocking specific number/date/person in the first 3 s, escalation every scene, an absurd or high stake, and a reveal the viewer cannot guess. Real and verifiable (two sources, low number) or clearly original fiction.

## Story families that fit (rotate; do not repeat a family back to back)
- Silent churn: a business/person lost customers for years and never knew why (real cases: lapsed customers, forgotten loyalty cards, abandoned subscriptions).
- Win-back miracles: one message/letter/call brought back someone who had gone (real reunions, returned items, refund-letter stories, 'we miss you' campaigns with measured results).
- Loyalty math: tiny repeat behaviors worth a fortune (Nunes & Dreze car wash, repeat customers vs new, 5% retention = big profit).
- Missed signals: the warning was there, nobody followed up (companies that ignored a lapsing client, a regular who just stopped coming).
- Reaching out first: a person who texted first and changed a life/business.
- Absurd history with a retention moral (promotion gimmicks, mail-in rebates nobody redeemed, a shop that survived on regulars).

## Avoid
Pure trivia with no link to keeping people (a flood, a rat poison marathon) unless the pivot is genuinely earned. Fake news about real brands. Barber/product talk before the last 10-12 s. Toy-world reels over 1 in 5.

## Per reel, record in the approve note
"moral: <one sentence>; pivot: <one line>; grip: <number/date>". Held or low-scoring stories that fail the test get replaced.
