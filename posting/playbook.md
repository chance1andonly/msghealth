## Testing system
See posting/experiments.md: rotate formats F1 to F7, judge after 48h on views, sends/reach and follows, replicate winners, kill after 3 misses. Keep one topic, vary the format.

## What the winners share
1. One human scene, a number or an age as the stake, outcome withheld: "He Waited 13 Years For A Haircut" (6.8M views on a 4,680-subscriber channel, 1.9% like rate); "I SHAVED HIS MOLE" (4.1M, 2.8% like rate, 17k subscribers). The title is the whole hook.
2. Verdict or vote devices keep people in the comments: vicblends ends with "vote below" and gets 25,894 comments on one reel; sami2fresh asks "Would you trust your barber to do this?".
3. A word-comment CTA that gives something: "comment LINK" (19.4M views, 2,410x the account's average). Ours is KEEP.
4. Questions about why clients leave ("Why do people often change barbers?", 1.7M views) transfer; we answer with a true story.
5. Does NOT transfer: free cuts for kids and veterans, ASMR, ingrown extractions, tutorials, Fresha and Booksy promos. Those win on emotion or craft, not on a business lesson.

## What changed this run
- Hooks stay one scene or number with the outcome withheld, but now the lesson line is a quoted text the barber can send, and the chat on screen repeats it word for word.
- Every fact checked against two or more pages. This run caught that the Chick-fil-A snow story was one store near Birmingham (not Atlanta, not many stores), so the script says one store; Blue Bell's phase and employee numbers conflicted across sources, so they were left out.
- Hook audio must stay under about 4.5s: if the line runs longer, cut words (Saturn's hook went from 14 to 10 words).

## Rules for new scripts
1. Hook = one odd true scene or number, outcome withheld, 14 words or fewer, under 4.5 seconds spoken.
2. Story first, lesson second; the payoff line before the CTA is the text the barber sends, and the chat shot shows that same text.
3. Where sources disagree, use the low number and attribute it, or drop it. Only state what two sources agree on.
4. Lines of 15 words or fewer; at most one "not X, it's Y"; no tidy aphorism closers.
5. Caption chunks on screen about 30 characters or fewer, two or three short lines.
6. Voice follows posting/VOICE.md; spell hard words phonetically in "say".

## People reels (about 1 in 3)
Some reels use `cast` scenes (animated people in a shop or street set, speech bubbles that match the spoken line) instead of
only stats and cards. A reel is a people reel when its random draw says so (see the batch task); in that reel at least 3 of
the 5 story scenes are `cast`, and the rest can be stat, versus or chat. Rules: one to three people on screen, a bubble line is
never longer than the spoken line it matches, people tell the scene (a stylist waving, a client on his phone, a chair that
stays empty) and the number or stake still appears as a chip. Keep the same look for the same person across scenes in one reel.

## Toy-world reels (the default look)
The channel's look is now low-poly 3D cartoons (src/toy.js, three.js): blocky toy people, barbershops, a lot, a road, night streets,
name tags over heads, giant yellow callout words, chat scenes, then the kit's end card. Reference: the chase4 video the owner uploaded
(an absurd chase story told in a toy world). Write the story as a scene: one shot per spoken line, one person doing one clear thing,
one callout word or tag per shot. Keep the comic, specific, absurd hook, and end on the text-him payoff and the KEEP CTA.
Shots: set shop|street|lot|road|night; actors p/car/crowd with poses idle walk run sit phone sitphone wave shock point cut sleep.
Camera notes (portrait, fov 58): stay 8 to 11 units from one or two people, 15 to 19 for wide shots; keep actors inside +-3 of the camera x.
Reference reel: src/toydre-reel.js. Set sky/grass per shot for snow (sky #c9d3dd, grass #eef3f8) or dawn (sky #f2a86e); crowds (k:crowd) take pose idle/wave/shock and are about 8 units wide, so put the camera 14 to 17 away. The renderer needs node_modules/playwright (symlink to the global one if the repo link is dangling); the Kokoro files live in /home/claude/models (kokoro-v1.0.onnx, voices-v1.0.bin from the kokoro-onnx GitHub release model-files-v1.0). Render takes about 4 minutes per reel.

## Toy-world reel format (viral AND explainer; this is the house style)
Reference look: the user's chase4 video: 3D toy world, one big white outlined WORD at a time in the middle of the screen, pink tilted name tags with a leader line, white counters at the top ("TEXTS SENT: 0"), a cut every 2-3 seconds, slow camera push. Engine: src/toy.js (word captions, tags, stats, chat UI, the Comment KEEP chip all built in).
Length 50-75 s. The Comment KEEP chip shows the whole video; KEEP is also spoken mid-story and at the end card. Say "Message Health" in the voice text (field "say"), show "MsgHealth" on screen (field "show"). "Dre" is spoken "Dray".
Shots are placed with at: [lineIndex, offsetSeconds] and a focus point f:[x,z] (d distance, h height, ly look height). Never move a character with a path while it is sitting or lying (it slides): only walk/run poses travel. Keep 1-2 people 6-9 units from the camera, house names short (sign width 6).
Props: door (falls), house/hospital buildings, bed, bowl of soup with bones, helicopter, truck, goat, car with siren, floating app panel, chat UI with BOOKED card. Poses: idle walk run sit phone wave shock point cut kick punch swing faint choke chokesit cheer cry slump hold.
Rendering: never run other heavy jobs while a reel renders (it makes the 3D floor glitch). After every render run python3 scripts/flicker.py on the mp4 and look at frames (python3 scripts/still.mjs / scripts/sheet.py) before approving.

### Viral reels (about 2 in 3 of a batch)
Goal: views and new audience. Profanity allowed (no slurs). Original archetypes only (no real people or copyrighted characters). Structure: HOOK = something happens that sets off a chain ("My client stopped coming in, so I kicked his fucking door down"), then each line escalates the absurdity (rule of three: mom, grandma, hospital), counters track the chaos, a deadpan reveal ("I just forgot"), the lesson ("nobody told him to come back"), the product in one line, "no door, no grandma, no soup, just a text", KEEP. A running joke gets a callback at the end (counters back to 0). Reference: src/toydoor-reel.js, src/toymissing-reel.js.

### Explainer reels (about 1 in 3 of a batch)
Goal: new followers understand what we sell. Facts only from posting/PRODUCT.md. Steps with counters (STEP 1: SCORE, STEP 2: TEXT, STEP 3: BOOK), the real app screens, light humor, no profanity, 35-50 s. Reference: src/toyexplain2-reel.js.

## Release gate (re-render if ANY of these fail)
A reel may only post if all three hold. If one fails, fix the script and re-render it (ads-vo.py, then render.mjs, then re-encode and approve again); if it can't be fixed, move it to posting/held/.
1. Every word is pronounced correctly. Listen to the ASR transcript from python3 scripts/qa.py NAME against the script; any name or word that comes out wrong (e.g. "Dre" must be spoken "Dray") gets respelled phonetically in the "say" text and rebuilt. Captions keep the real spelling.
2. Every frame is properly defined. Look at a frame for each shot (ffmpeg contact sheet): subject in frame and not cropped, nothing overlapping the caption zone (callouts at y about 520 or 1240), text readable, no empty or broken shot. Fix the camera, actors or callout and re-render.
3. It is predicted to beat the previous videos' analytics. Compare against posting/stats.json (top_reels, last posts): hook type, style, length, topic and caption shape must match what is winning, and the hook must be at least as strong as the best recent reel. If not, rewrite the hook or script and re-render rather than posting a weaker reel.
