# Niche playbook (the batch task rewrites this from posting/research.json and posting/stats.json)

Updated 2026-11-21 (research.json 2026-11-21, stats.json 2026-11-18, 45 reels, no new posted data since). Judged from titles, captions and numbers only; I did not watch the videos.

## What the winners share
1. One human scene, outcome withheld, a person's age or years as the stake: "He Waited 13 Years For A Haircut" (6.3M views, about 1,700x the channel's subscribers, 1.9% like rate); vicblends' free cut for a 10 year old and a 102 year old veteran (about 10.8M views each, comment rates up to 4%).
2. Question titles about why clients leave: "Why do people often change barbers?" (1.7M views, 1.65% like rate). The structure transfers; our version answers it with a true story.
3. A word-comment CTA that gives something: the "comment LINK" reel (19.4M views). Ours is KEEP.
4. A vote or verdict in the caption ("vote below", check or cross) keeps people in the comments.
5. Does NOT transfer: free cuts for strangers, kids and veterans (consumer feel-good), ASMR, ingrown extractions, Fresha/Booksy promo posts.

## Rules for new scripts
1. Hook = one odd true scene or number, outcome withheld, 14 words or fewer, under 4.5 seconds spoken.
2. Story first, lesson second; the payoff sits in the line before the text, and the text line is the barber's action.
3. Where sources disagree, use the low number and attribute it ("Squire says", "per Selling Power"). Skip anything only one blocked or vendor source claims, unless attributed.
4. Lines of 15 words or fewer; at most one "not X, it's Y"; no "every time" closers.
5. Caption chunks on screen about 30 characters or fewer, 3 short lines.
6. Voice follows posting/VOICE.md.

## Captions
- Line 1 is the scene or number from the video, one emoji. Then the lesson in two sentences, a question or check/cross verdict, the CTA "Comment KEEP and we'll show you who's gone quiet.", then #barber #atlantabarber #hairstylist.
- No em dashes, no cliches (posting/VOICE.md).

## People reels (about 1 in 3)
Some reels use `cast` scenes (animated people in a shop or street set, speech bubbles that match the spoken line) instead of
only stats and cards. A reel is a people reel when its random draw says so (see the batch task); in that reel at least 3 of
the 5 story scenes are `cast`, and the rest can be stat, versus or chat. Rules: one to three people on screen, a bubble line is
never longer than the spoken line it matches, people tell the scene (a stylist waving, a client on his phone, a chair that
stays empty) and the number or stake still appears as a chip. Keep the same look for the same person across scenes in one reel.
